# Hook pattern library (brand-agnostic)

Reusable `[x]` hook templates for any brand, grouped by opener type. Each has a one-line why and an any-brand example. Fill the brackets with the target brand's problem, identity, and outcome.

---

## qualifier (13)
- **Pattern:** "I'm a [specific identity], which means [vivid daily reality]."
  - Why: Hyper-specific identity head-nod so the right viewer feels seen before the problem lands.
  - Any-brand example: I'm a night-shift nurse, which means I eat dinner at 4am.
- **Pattern:** "If you [problem A], [problem B], whatever it is, this is for you."
  - Why: Stacks a couple of problem variants so anyone with any version self-selects in.
  - Any-brand example: If you overspend, undersave, whatever it is, this is for you.
- **Pattern:** "I'm a [identity], which means [hardship 1], [hardship 2], and [the problem]."
  - Why: Identity head-nod that piles on relatable hardships before naming the problem.
  - Any-brand example: I'm a founder, which means no weekends, no off switch, and no real focus.
- **Pattern:** "If you [symptom], [symptom], [symptom], here's what finally helped."
  - Why: Agitation stack: lists symptom after symptom to twist the knife, then the payoff.
  - Any-brand example: If you bloat after every meal, feel heavy, can't button your jeans by 3pm, here's what fixed it.
- **Pattern:** "If you're [demographic] and still [problem], you might be [doing the fix wrong]."
  - Why: Demographic qualifier plus 'you're doing it wrong' to hook people who already tried a fix.
  - Any-brand example: If you're over 40 and still breaking out, you might be moisturizing wrong.
- **Pattern:** "If you're [demographic] and [problem], you can thank me now."
  - Why: Demographic qualifier with a confident 'you can thank me now' payoff promise.
  - Any-brand example: If you're over 30 and your metabolism tanked, you can thank me later.
- **Pattern:** "These are made for [specific identity]."
  - Why: Blunt 'made for [identity]' claim that self-selects the tribe.
  - Any-brand example: This was built for people who hate meal prep.
- **Pattern:** "I'm [identity], so when I [use product] I need it to [spec 1], [spec 2], [spec 3]."
  - Why: Identity plus a hard spec list the product must hit.
  - Any-brand example: I travel weekly, so my bag needs to fit a laptop, last a week, and never get checked.
- **Pattern:** "It's [time of day] and if you're [identity] like me, you're probably [paradoxical feeling]."
  - Why: Time-of-day plus identity plus the 'wired and tired' paradox language.
  - Any-brand example: It's 4pm and if you work from home like me, you're probably wired and foggy at once.
- **Pattern:** "Every one of my friends [has this problem]. Everyone [demographic]."
  - Why: Consensus qualifier: everyone in my circle has this, in this age band.
  - Any-brand example: Every friend I have is burnt out. Every single one in their 30s.
- **Pattern:** "If you're [doing exact thing] right now because [problem], first [playful callout], second, try this."
  - Why: Fourth-wall, real-time callout to the person scrolling in bed right now.
  - Any-brand example: If you're doom-scrolling at 1am right now, first go to bed, second, save this.
- **Pattern:** "Raise your hand if you're [identity] who [hyper-specific problem]."
  - Why: 'Raise your hand if' plus a hyper-specific frustrating symptom.
  - Any-brand example: Raise your hand if you're a parent who hasn't peed alone since 2019.
- **Pattern:** "[Partner/ally], if you think [downplayed version], you're misunderstanding what [real experience] is like."
  - Why: Advocate POV: addresses the partner/ally and reframes the downplayed problem.
  - Any-brand example: Managers, if you think your new hire is just quiet, you're missing what onboarding anxiety looks like.

## confession (15)
- **Pattern:** "Ever since [life event], I haven't been able to [basic function]."
  - Why: New-parent sleep loss framed as an ongoing state, not a one-off.
  - Any-brand example: Ever since I turned 30, my skin stopped bouncing back overnight.
- **Pattern:** "My dumbass has tried every [category] under the sun, and landed on the [few] that actually work."
  - Why: Self-deprecating 'my dumbass tried everything' that bonds with the frustrated buyer.
  - Any-brand example: My broke self tried every cheap mascara out there, here are the two that don't smudge.
- **Pattern:** "Please tell me I'm not alone: [vulnerable admission]."
  - Why: Me-too vulnerability that fishes for 'omg same' in the comments.
  - Any-brand example: Please tell me I'm not the only one who still can't do a real push-up at 35.
- **Pattern:** "I'm officially so [old/whatever] that I now have to [embarrassing behavior]."
  - Why: Self-deprecating aging bit that's really a relatable head-nod.
  - Any-brand example: I'm officially so old that I get excited about a good pillow.
- **Pattern:** "I just survived [relatable daily battle], here's what I do to [recover]."
  - Why: Names the relatable daily battle, then pivots to the wind-down demo.
  - Any-brand example: I just survived the daycare-drop-off sprint, here's my 3-minute reset.
- **Pattern:** "I'm so mad at myself, I bought [multiple things] when I could've just bought [this one]."
  - Why: Regret confession that doubles as a price/efficacy argument.
  - Any-brand example: I'm so mad I bought 4 serums when this one drugstore bottle did it all.
- **Pattern:** "I used to be able to [past-self behavior], now I [current-self behavior]."
  - Why: Aging bit with a specific, funny image (the Irish goodbye).
  - Any-brand example: I used to close the bar, now I'm mentally leaving the party during appetizers.
- **Pattern:** "I'm [going through condition], and I'm really going through it. One symptom nobody warns you about..."
  - Why: States the condition plainly, then opens a loop on a specific symptom.
  - Any-brand example: I'm in my first trimester and wow. One symptom no one mentions...
- **Pattern:** "I try to sleep, but then I remember [random embarrassing memory from years ago]."
  - Why: Overthinking-at-night bit: a random old memory hijacks bedtime.
  - Any-brand example: I try to sleep and my brain goes: remember that thing you said in 2011?
- **Pattern:** "[Problem] is keeping me [wordplay state]."
  - Why: Punchy wordplay confession (insomnia/insane) that names the feeling.
  - Any-brand example: Decision fatigue is making me useless.
- **Pattern:** "Nobody warned me about [x]."
  - Why: Portable 'nobody warned me' opener for any unexpected downside.
  - Any-brand example: Nobody warned me about postpartum hair loss.
- **Pattern:** "I've been [annoying someone] for [long time]. How? [reveal the mundane cause]."
  - Why: Long-running relatable grievance, then an implied-answer 'how exactly?'
  - Any-brand example: I've been driving my wife crazy for a decade. How? I snore like a chainsaw.
- **Pattern:** "I'm so [state] that I [absurd specific evidence]. So there's that."
  - Why: Self-deprecating proof of exhaustion via one absurd, specific detail.
  - Any-brand example: I'm so scattered lately I put my keys in the freezer. So, yeah.
- **Pattern:** "I woke up full of rage at [person] for being able to [thing I can't]."
  - Why: Relatable rage at the person who can do the thing you can't (sleep).
  - Any-brand example: I woke up furious at my partner for being able to fall asleep in four minutes.
- **Pattern:** "Mostly I'm fine, but then I think back to that time I [specific embarrassing mistake]."
  - Why: Opens calm, then drops a funny, specific self-own tied to the exact problem the product solves.
  - Any-brand example: Mostly I'm fine, but then I remember the time I hit reply-all to the entire company.
  - Origin: https://www.tiktok.com/@missta_babythermos/video/7656740163875392788

## flex (3)
- **Pattern:** "I don't know what's in this, but I've been [surprising positive result]."
  - Why: Fresh result comparison ('sleeping like my husband, better than a baby') plus mystery.
  - Any-brand example: I don't know what's in this, but my under-eyes look like I slept 9 hours.
- **Pattern:** "I've been having the best [result] I've had in I don't even know how long."
  - Why: Understated result flex that opens a 'how' loop.
  - Any-brand example: I've had the best skin days I've had in years.
- **Pattern:** "I'm [age/hard circumstance], and I [have the result everyone wants]."
  - Why: Age-plus-hard-circumstance flex on the exact result the audience wants.
  - Any-brand example: I'm 52 with two bad knees, and I just ran my first 10k.

## open loop (6)
- **Pattern:** "You might be wondering why I'm [unusual thing]. It's because [reason tied to problem]."
  - Why: Opens on an odd visual (pyjamas, middle of the night) and withholds the why.
  - Any-brand example: You might be wondering why I'm filming from my closet floor. It's the only quiet spot.
- **Pattern:** "If you already [have the ideal state], this isn't for you, keep scrolling."
  - Why: Reverse qualifier: names the ideal state and tells the lucky few to scroll on.
  - Any-brand example: If your inbox is at zero and you never feel behind, this isn't for you.
- **Pattern:** "I need to tell you about [product] that's going to change the game for [specific problem]."
  - Why: 'I need to tell you about this' announcement that opens a curiosity gap.
  - Any-brand example: I need to tell you about the tool that killed my 5pm inbox anxiety.
- **Pattern:** "Nobody's talking about this, but if you're [identity], you need to [action]."
  - Why: 'Nobody's talking about this' scarcity-of-info plus an identity callout.
  - Any-brand example: Nobody's talking about this, but if you sit all day, you need this one stretch.
- **Pattern:** "No one told me [x] would fix [y] too."
  - Why: Bonus-benefit reveal that opens a curiosity gap.
  - Any-brand example: No one told me creatine would help my mood too.
- **Pattern:** "I think I had a [topic] breakthrough."
  - Why: 'I think I had a breakthrough' teases a payoff worth staying for.
  - Any-brand example: I think I had a productivity breakthrough.

## question (3)
- **Pattern:** "Why is it that I'm [state] all day, but the second [trigger], I'm [opposite]?"
  - Why: Relatable paradox as a question: exhausted all day, wired at bedtime.
  - Any-brand example: Why am I starving all afternoon, but the second dinner's ready, I'm not hungry?
- **Pattern:** "Have you ever wondered why your [problem] is so [blunt descriptor]?"
  - Why: Blunt rhetorical question that says exactly what the viewer is thinking.
  - Any-brand example: Have you ever wondered why your energy is so all over the place?
- **Pattern:** "Is there anything [problem/condition] doesn't mess with?"
  - Why: Exasperated rhetorical question that everyone with the problem nods at.
  - Any-brand example: Is there anything a newborn doesn't turn upside down?

## rhetorical question (4)
- **Pattern:** "Does [popular ingredient] actually work for [goal]? I'm a [credential], let's talk."
  - Why: Myth-test question that a credentialed voice then answers.
  - Any-brand example: Does collagen actually help your joints? I'm a physio, let's talk.
- **Pattern:** "Everyone says [common claim], but is that actually true?"
  - Why: Challenges a widely repeated claim and promises to settle it.
  - Any-brand example: Everyone says you need 10k steps a day, but is that actually true?
- **Pattern:** "Did you know [surprising mechanism about a daily habit]?"
  - Why: 'Did you know' that reframes an everyday habit as the hidden cause.
  - Any-brand example: Did you know skipping breakfast can spike your afternoon cravings?
- **Pattern:** "Respectfully, [my situation], I need more than [the obvious advice]."
  - Why: Rhetorical rebuttal to unsolicited advice, standing in for the viewer's own eye-roll.
  - Any-brand example: Respectfully, I have 3 clients and a newborn, I need more than a to-do list app.

## listicle (6)
- **Pattern:** "This is my [routine/stack] that helps me [primary benefit], but not only that, [secondary benefit]."
  - Why: Reveals the full stack, then adds a second benefit on top of the obvious one.
  - Any-brand example: This is my wash-day routine that kills the frizz, and it halved my styling time.
- **Pattern:** "Here's how to know if you should be [taking/doing X]."
  - Why: Teach-y 'here's how to know if' that promises quick self-diagnosis.
  - Any-brand example: Here's how to know if you actually need creatine.
- **Pattern:** "The reason [problem] happens is because [surprising cause]."
  - Why: Mechanism reveal that names the cause behind the problem.
  - Any-brand example: The reason you crash at 2pm is because of what you ate at 8am.
- **Pattern:** "[N] things I wish I knew when I [had the problem]."
  - Why: Classic '[N] things I wish I knew' teach with a problem-state hook.
  - Any-brand example: 5 things I wish I knew when I started lifting.
- **Pattern:** "Here are [N] things that fixed my [problem] as a [identity with ironic twist]."
  - Why: Listicle with an ironic-twist identity ('baby sleeps, now I can't').
  - Any-brand example: 5 things that fixed my energy as the person who quit coffee and got more tired.
- **Pattern:** "My [routine] is [absurd stack of items], and I regret nothing."
  - Why: Absurd routine stack played for 'and I regret nothing' comedy.
  - Any-brand example: My focus setup is noise-cancelers, a white-noise app, and a hoodie up indoors, and I regret nothing.

## time-box (1)
- **Pattern:** "In the next [short time], I'm going to teach you [outcome]."
  - Why: Names a short, low-commitment time cost up front so the viewer agrees to stay for the payoff.
  - Any-brand example: In the next 30 seconds I'll show you how to cut your grocery bill in half.

## credentials-first (6)
- **Pattern:** "Here's the correct way to [do thing], from a [credential]'s perspective."
  - Why: Leads with the credential so the how-to carries authority.
  - Any-brand example: Here's the right way to wash your face, from a dermatologist's perspective.
- **Pattern:** "These are the [products] I reach for most as a [credential]."
  - Why: Credential plus identity as the reason-to-believe on a product pick.
  - Any-brand example: These are the 3 supplements I take daily as a longevity doctor.
- **Pattern:** "I'm a [credential], and this is my top pick if you want [vivid outcome]."
  - Why: Pharmacist identity plus a vivid outcome name ('anaesthesia-level sleep').
  - Any-brand example: I'm a hairstylist, and this is my top pick if you want glass-like shine.
- **Pattern:** "Here's how to [do thing] correctly. I'm a [credential], it's literally my job."
  - Why: 'It's literally my job' makes the advice feel like an insider duty.
  - Any-brand example: Here's how to actually use a foam roller. I'm a PT, it's literally my job.
- **Pattern:** "I'm a [credential], and even I deal with [the problem]."
  - Why: Credentialed voice admits they have the problem too, which builds trust.
  - Any-brand example: I'm a personal trainer, and even I lose motivation in January.
- **Pattern:** "I'll tell you how to [x], but you have to hear me out, and here are my credentials."
  - Why: Asks for the viewer's full attention, then flashes proof (the credentials).
  - Any-brand example: I'll tell you how to fix your posture, but hear me out, and here's my proof.

## guinea pig (4)
- **Pattern:** "I've tried every [category] you can think of, and here's my honest verdict."
  - Why: Exhaustive-trial credibility: tried everything, here's the honest verdict.
  - Any-brand example: I've tried every project app you can think of, here's the only one I kept.
- **Pattern:** "I'm going to [do the thing] for [time period] so you don't have to."
  - Why: Time-boxed self-test with a built-in reason to keep watching to the payoff.
  - Any-brand example: I drank celery juice every morning for 30 days so you don't have to.
- **Pattern:** "I stopped [using product] for [time] to see if it was placebo. Here's what happened."
  - Why: Placebo self-test: stops using the product to prove it was real.
  - Any-brand example: I stopped using my eye cream for a week to see if it did anything. Big mistake.
- **Pattern:** "I just bought [product] off an ad to see if it actually [claim]. Testing it now."
  - Why: Live test of a product bought off an ad, curiosity built in.
  - Any-brand example: I bought that viral posture corrector off an ad, let's see if it does anything.

## POV (1)
- **Pattern:** "POV: you're [absurdly specific relatable behavior]."
  - Why: POV opener on an absurdly specific, instantly recognizable behavior.
  - Any-brand example: POV: you're cutting a pre-workout scoop in half because you value your heart rate.

## metaphor (2)
- **Pattern:** "If you want to feel like [vivid sensory metaphor], here are my [topic] tips."
  - Why: Sensory metaphor ('sleeping in the clouds') plus the culturally live term sleepmaxxing.
  - Any-brand example: If you want to feel like you're floating on water, here's my setup.
- **Pattern:** "When I [try to do X], it's like [vivid metaphor] turns on and I can't shut it off."
  - Why: Mechanical metaphor ('check engine light in my brain') for the racing mind.
  - Any-brand example: The second I sit down to focus, it's like every browser tab in my brain opens at once.

## series (1)
- **Pattern:** "Welcome to day [N] of the [topic] survival guide."
  - Why: Implies an established series ('day 3 of the survival guide') for borrowed credibility.
  - Any-brand example: Welcome to day 4 of the toddler-tantrum survival guide.

## discovery (2)
- **Pattern:** "I thought [condition] was just [known symptom], but then I typed '[the exact thing I'd google]' into ChatGPT. Apparently it's also [condition]."
  - Why: Broken mental model sends them to search, and the answer reframes the problem.
  - Any-brand example: I thought burnout was just being tired, but then I googled 'why do I feel nothing at work.' Oh.
- **Pattern:** "When [scary symptom] hit at [age], I was convinced I had [worst-case]. Turns out it was [real cause]."
  - Why: Misdiagnosis journey: feared the worst, discovered the real cause.
  - Any-brand example: When my heart raced every morning, I was sure it was my heart. Turns out it was cortisol.

## apology (1)
- **Pattern:** "I'd like to formally apologize to [group I doubted], I was not familiar with your game."
  - Why: Mock formal apology to a group she used to doubt, disarming and funny.
  - Any-brand example: I owe the meal-prep girlies an apology. I was not familiar with your game.

## PSA (5)
- **Pattern:** "If you [common behavior], please listen, this is something I wish I knew."
  - Why: Heads-up aimed at existing users of the product, framed as an inside tip.
  - Any-brand example: If you use retinol at night, please listen, I wish someone told me this.
- **Pattern:** "Do not [use this] unless you're absolutely ready to [strong effect]."
  - Why: Reverse-warning: 'do not use this unless you're ready for the effect.'
  - Any-brand example: Do not take this pre-workout unless you're ready to actually finish your list.
- **Pattern:** "[Identity], do not sleep on [thing]."
  - Why: Direct address plus a pun command ('don't sleep on sleep').
  - Any-brand example: Founders, do not sleep on your onboarding flow.
- **Pattern:** "[Experiential credential] here. [Do the thing]. Just [expletive] do it."
  - Why: Experiential authority plus a blunt, funny command.
  - Any-brand example: Third startup here. Talk to your users. Just actually do it.
- **Pattern:** "Whatever you do, do not accidentally [x], it will [effect]. What you want is [right way]."
  - Why: Reverse-psychology warning that bakes the benefit into the 'don't.'
  - Any-brand example: Whatever you do, don't accidentally take this on an empty stomach, it hits fast.

## reframe (6)
- **Pattern:** "As a [identity], I used to think [belief], but I've learned [inverted belief]."
  - Why: Flips a limiting belief the target already holds (the fix felt pointless, actually matters).
  - Any-brand example: As a renter, I used to think decorating was a waste, but it changes the whole day.
- **Pattern:** "I don't have a [surface] problem, I have a [real] problem."
  - Why: Reframes the surface complaint as the real underlying problem.
  - Any-brand example: I don't have a motivation problem, I have a sleep problem.
- **Pattern:** "I don't [milder version]. I [worse, escalated version]."
  - Why: Reversal that escalates the problem past the milder version.
  - Any-brand example: I don't procrastinate. I'm still not started at midnight.
- **Pattern:** "My first clue wasn't [expected symptom]. It was [surprising one]."
  - Why: First-clue reversal: the surprising early sign, not the expected one.
  - Any-brand example: My first sign of burnout wasn't exhaustion. It was not caring about things I loved.
- **Pattern:** "[Thing] isn't [flattering label], but it sure as hell isn't [harsh label]."
  - Why: Stakes out an honest middle by negating both extremes, which reads as credible instead of salesy.
  - Any-brand example: Our coffee isn't fancy, but it sure as hell isn't gas-station sludge.
  - Origin: https://www.tiktok.com/@amber.figlow/video/7638618829999836429
- **Pattern:** "If you're not [desired state], it's because you're not [blamed cause]. And you and I both know that's BS."
  - Why: Rage-bait: voices the guilt-trip narrative the viewer hears from others or themselves, then flips to their side.
  - Any-brand example: If you're not rich, it's because you're not hustling hard enough. And you and I both know that's BS.
  - Origin: https://www.tiktok.com/@emmagrede/video/7615612480345148703

## preempted objection (3)
- **Pattern:** "If [x] isn't helping with [y], you're probably [doing z]."
  - Why: Answers the 'I tried it and it didn't work' objection with a reason why.
  - Any-brand example: If magnesium isn't helping you sleep, you're probably taking the wrong form.
- **Pattern:** "If [x] did nothing for [y], watch this before you give up on it."
  - Why: Catches people right before they give up on the product.
  - Any-brand example: If retinol did nothing for your texture, watch this before you quit it.
- **Pattern:** "If you're the victim of [failed solution] that promised you [result], I promise this is different."
  - Why: Names the failed solution that burned them before raising the new one.
  - Any-brand example: If you got burned by every fad diet that promised abs, I promise this is different.

## borrowed enemy (2)
- **Pattern:** "I've learned more about [problem] from [social] than from my own [expert], and the one thing that changed everything is..."
  - Why: Positions the doctor/institution as the enemy, then opens a payoff loop.
  - Any-brand example: I've learned more about money from TikTok than from my own bank, and one thing changed everything.
- **Pattern:** "They didn't warn us about [earlier thing], so no wonder they didn't warn us about [current thing]."
  - Why: Blames the 'they' who never prepared us, uniting the audience in grievance.
  - Any-brand example: They never taught us taxes in school, so of course no one warned us about health insurance.

## contrast (1)
- **Pattern:** "This is my [metric] before. This is my [metric] now."
  - Why: Shows the wearable score before vs now and lets the jump make the argument.
  - Any-brand example: This is my resting heart rate before. This is it now.

## social witness (1)
- **Pattern:** "[Someone] pointed out I used to [past behavior]. They weren't being rude, they were right."
  - Why: A friend's offhand comment surfaces the problem from the outside.
  - Any-brand example: My sister said I used to text back in seconds. She wasn't wrong, the brain fog is real.

## demonstration (1)
- **Pattern:** "I didn't think [product] was doing anything, until I ran out."
  - Why: Proof by absence: didn't believe it worked until they ran out.
  - Any-brand example: I didn't think my magnesium was doing anything, until I traveled without it.
